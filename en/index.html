AI Cultural Tourism · RAG Retrieval & Source Attribution
Ancient Architecture Discovery · AI Cultural-Tourism RAG Q&A Platform National 2nd Prize
First Author / Lead · Mar 2026 – Jul 2026 · China Collegiate Computing Design Competition
General-purpose LLMs fabricate eras and architectural forms when asked about historic buildings. I turned hundreds of academic papers into a domain knowledge base of 2,001 chunks, with a retrieval pipeline that ties every answer back to a traceable source.
  • Retrieval: hand-written BM25 (k1=1.5, b=0.75); Chinese text split by bigrams to boost recall without depending on a tokenizer
  • Re-ranking: BM25 score combined with multi-way weighting over metadata (building name / province / category), deduplicated down to Top-5
  • Entity normalization: an alias table collapses "Forbidden City / Zijin Cheng / Hall of Supreme Harmony" into a single entity
  • Why not a vector store: at 2,001-chunk scale, BM25 recall is already sufficient — and it's GPU-free, interpretable, and has zero cold-start cost
  • Interaction: MediaPipe gesture control over a "knowledge nebula," one-click Chinese/English toggle, scene-based AI-generated background music
  • Evaluation: on 100 manually labeled questions, BM25 + metadata re-ranking reached 86% Recall@5, an 11-point gain over plain BM25; manual review found 89% of answers had their key claims supported by the linked sources
  • Campus rollout: 192 new users on launch day, 62% of them international students

Jikexing · Guangzhou Cultural-Tourism Recommendation System
Project Lead · 10-person team · University Innovation Program / "Challenge Cup" university award
Guangzhou's core attractions run 33% over capacity daily; the social-platform information tourists rely on for planning is only 36% trustworthy, and OTA recommendations are 79% homogeneous. The product breaks "where to go, when, how to get there" into a computable decision problem: real-time crowding, attraction ratings, weather, and holidays are combined into a "quality index" that generates a personalized itinerary for each visitor.
  • Chose weighted scoring over a complex model: crowding 40% + attraction rating 30% + real-time weather 20% + holiday tag 10% generates "today's picks" — a deliberate engineering trade-off to avoid the compute cost and cold-start problems of a complex model

  • Planned: a multi-influence POI embedding model at the core, using a time-series model to forecast crowding 2 hours ahead, plus an LLM for "chat → analyze → generate" itinerary customization

Method (DCM-GAN): generative ZSL typically replaces manually annotated attributes with CLIP class-name embeddings as the semantic condition to cut annotation cost — but class-name semantics only encode high-level category information and lack fine-grained visual cues, causing synthesized features to be loosely clustered within a class and blurred across class boundaries. Dynamic Concept Memory (DCM) mines transferable visual concepts from CLIP local tokens on seen classes to strengthen weak semantics; the Contrastive Generation Network (CGN) applies dual contrastive constraints on top of a conditional f-WGAN.
My contribution: proposed "contrastive feature generation," one of the paper's two headline contributions — conceived and implemented independently after reading the relevant literature. At the instance level, a supervised contrastive loss (two-layer MLP projection head + temperature τ) tightens the intra-class distribution; at the class level, augmented semantics are mapped through a learnable linear layer into class prototypes for prototype contrast, forcing generated features to align with class semantics. Also ran training, hyperparameter search, and ablations on the CUB and AWA2 benchmarks.

CAVS: Calibrated Adaptive Visual-Semantic Learning for Balanced Generalized Zero-Shot Recognition
Dian Yu (1st author), Xiaoqin Lin, Min Meng (corresponding)　·　School of Computer Science, Guangdong University of Technology
Journal
Pattern Recognition Letters (Elsevier, SCI)
Status
Under review (Manuscript No. PRLETTERS-D-26-01626)
Setup
ImageNet-1K pretrained ViT-B/16, single RTX 4080
Problem: strong ViT-based methods on GZSL show a seen/unseen accuracy gap of over 20 points. On a self-built dual-branch ViT-B/16 baseline, I verified this gap is not a capacity issue (this baseline already reaches 77.4% H on AWA2, higher than SVIP, ICCV'25), and traced the cause to three coupled factors: asymmetric visual-semantic alignment, redundant dual-branch decisions, and a fixed, sample-agnostic calibration bias.
Method: rather than scaling up the backbone, I designed one module per cause — DSAM applies a bidirectional symmetric constraint in attribute space, BCD explicitly decorrelates the two branches' decisions, and ADBC replaces the fixed global bias with an end-to-end learned, sample-level calibration.
